By most measures, the music industry is larger and more fragmented than at any point in its history. Chartmetric's annual report is one of the more detailed independent, data-based views of how it is changing.

This article summarizes the main findings from its 2023 data.

---

## Artist Growth: Lower Barriers to Entry

More than **1.3 million new artists** debuted on streaming platforms in 2023. The cost of recording, distributing, and promoting music has fallen to the point where nearly anyone can release it.

The distribution of attention is highly skewed: most new artists reach very small audiences, and a small share account for most listening. The long tail still matters, because it is the pool from which future successful artists emerge, and discovery within it increasingly happens through algorithms as well as human curators.

Rather than flattening, the genre landscape is becoming more detailed. Niche genres such as "Sad Girl Indie" and "Cutecore Rap" show measurable traction on streaming platforms, reflecting further fragmentation into smaller audiences that are reached differently than mass-market audiences.

## The Volume Problem: 17 Million Tracks

Chartmetric tracked over **17 million new tracks** ingested in 2023. As the report notes, a single listener could not get through one year of uploads in a century of continuous listening.

For artists, the supply of music is no longer the constraint; listener attention is.

For data analysts and strategists working in the space, this volume creates both an opportunity and a challenge:

## Platform Landscape

Spotify remains central to music streaming and discovery, but the ecosystem has diversified:

- **YouTube** drives substantial discovery, particularly for international audiences and older listeners
- **TikTok** is a major source of viral music moments, though industry analyses find that short-form virality does not reliably translate into sustained streaming
- **Radio** retains significant reach, particularly in markets where mobile data costs limit streaming adoption

As a result, an artist's data footprint is spread across platforms. Part of Chartmetric's role is aggregating these signals into one analytics layer, which mirrors the challenge data engineers face in any industry where consumer behavior spans multiple channels.

## What This Means for Music Business Strategy

Several implications follow from the 2023 data:

**Catalog depth.** With 17 million new tracks a year, a single release rarely sustains revenue on its own. Catalog music, meaning older releases, accounts for a large and growing share of streaming consumption, which gives artists and labels with deep catalogs more stable income.

**Emerging markets.** Platform adoption is growing fastest in Latin America, Africa, and Southeast Asia. Streaming has lowered the geographic barriers that once shaped how artists expanded internationally.

**Virality versus longevity.** Several 2023 viral hits followed a pattern of a sharp short-term spike followed by rapid decline. Sustained streaming success and short-form virality appear to depend on different factors, which makes measuring them separately important.

---

## The Analyst's Takeaway

Chartmetric's data illustrates how central data has become to the music industry. Distribution, discovery, audience development, and pricing decisions increasingly rely on it.

Creative quality remains the foundation, and data is increasingly used alongside it to understand how music finds and keeps an audience.
