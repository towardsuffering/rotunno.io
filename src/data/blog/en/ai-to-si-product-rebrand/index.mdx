On September 29, 2026, the President signed **Executive Order 14434, "Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence."** From now on, federal agencies are to say **"Super Intelligence"** and **"SI"** instead of "Artificial Intelligence" and "AI," and the order says the executive branch "will not acknowledge the usage" of the old terms "in any applicable setting."

I'll admit I'm an early adopter. My own title on this site already reads *SI Orchestration*.

But walk into any electronics store and count the AIs. They're on the phones, the laptops, the TVs, the washers, and the fridges. So here's my question: **how long until the box says SI too?** My guess: not until there's something new inside it.

---

## What the Order Actually Does

The order is short, about two pages in the Federal Register. Here's the whole thing in four lines:

- **Who it covers:** executive departments and agencies, in "official correspondence, public communications, websites, reports, policy documents," and similar documents.
- **What it leaves alone:** existing regulations, contracts, grants, and other historical documents don't have to be rewritten.
- **What SI means, for now:** exactly what AI already means under federal law (15 U.S.C. § 9401(3)). Same technology, new name.
- **What comes next:** within 60 days, the President's science and technology adviser has to send proposed *legislative* language for a new federal definition. Only Congress can change the law's wording.

What it doesn't do matters just as much. As lawyers at Arnold & Porter put it, the order "imposes no obligations on private parties" and "has no penalty or enforcement provision." **Nobody is making Samsung reprint anything.**

---

## AI Is Baked Into the Box

Over the last few years, "AI" stopped being a feature and became part of product names.

- **Phones:** Samsung launched **Galaxy AI** with the Galaxy S24 in January 2024. Apple answered with **Apple Intelligence**, which conveniently shortens to... AI.
- **Laptops:** Microsoft created a whole PC category called **Copilot+ PC** in May 2024 (that's one on the cover of this post), and the industry calls the class "AI PCs."
- **Home appliances:** Samsung sells **Bespoke AI** refrigerators and washers, and its CES 2026 theme was "Your Companion to AI Living."

## Why Nobody Relabels the Fridge

If a company swapped "AI" for "SI" on the same fridge tomorrow, what would a shopper see? The same fridge, with a sticker. A few reasons to expect companies to wait:

1. **Nobody has to.** The order covers the government, not the private sector.
2. **"AI" is what shoppers search for.** Years of ads taught people what AI means. "SI" doesn't have that recognition yet, and it already means other things, from the metric system's units to Sports Illustrated.
3. **The world still says AI.** These companies sell globally, and laws like the EU's AI Act are written around the term "AI system." A U.S.-only label is a second set of packaging.
4. **Renaming without a reason backfires.** In December 2017, Long Island Iced Tea Corp. renamed itself Long Blockchain Corp. The stock jumped, Nasdaq delisted it about two months later, and the SEC later brought insider trading charges tied to the announcement. A new name with nothing new behind it reads as a gimmick.

---

## The Next Product Generation Is the Window

Names usually change when the product does. The "cell phone" became a "smartphone" when it learned to browse the web and run apps. TVs went from HD to 4K when the panel actually had more pixels.

SI will probably follow the same path. My prediction: **the first "SI" product won't be a relabeled AI product. It'll be a new category**, one that does something the AI version couldn't: an assistant that finishes a task on its own instead of suggesting one, or a home that runs itself instead of answering questions about itself.

Some companies already have the word ready. Meta named its lab **Meta Superintelligence Labs** in 2025 and published a vision for "personal superintelligence." When the capability arrives, the name is already waiting.

So I'll be watching the next big product cycles, starting with CES 2027 in January:

- **Who goes first?** A challenger brand with nothing to lose, or a giant that wants to own the term?
- **Does SI show up as a product name or a tier?** Think "Pro" or "Ultra": *Galaxy SI* above *Galaxy AI*.
- **Will Congress's definition actually differ from AI's?** If it does, "SI" gets a legal meaning, and companies will have to earn the label.

---

## What I'm Taking Away

An executive order can change what the government calls something. It can't change what's on the shelf. Companies will move to SI when it helps them sell a product, and that will take something new to sell.

I use SI in my own title because it describes how I work today: I design the system, SI builds it, and I make sure it works. Brands will make the switch when their products can say the same thing honestly.

So, a question for you: **what would a product need to do before you'd believe the "SI" on the box?**
