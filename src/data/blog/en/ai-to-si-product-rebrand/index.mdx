Look closely at the cover. Those are LG washers and dryers, and the badge above them says **"Deep Learning AI DD."** That's artificial intelligence, certified, on a laundry machine.

Now the vocabulary is changing. **Super Intelligence, or SI**, is the new term in Washington, and some of tech's biggest names have picked it up. I use it myself (my title on this site reads *SI Orchestration*).

But that washer still says AI, and it will for a while. My take is simple: **companies can't just snap their fingers. SI will arrive with the next evolution of products.**

---

## Why Not Just Rename It?

### The name is built into the product

"AI" isn't a sticker. It's in product names (**Galaxy AI**, **Bespoke AI**, **Apple Intelligence**), in the chips (**Samsung's NQ8 AI Gen3 Processor**), and on certification badges like the one on the cover. Renaming means new packaging, new ads, new on-screen menus, new store listings, and in some cases new certifications.

### Products are planned years ahead

Electronics run on cycles. TV makers unveil each year's lineup at CES in January, and phones refresh once a year. The products on shelves today were designed long before anyone in Washington said "SI."

### A new name needs something new behind it

Customers notice when a label promises nothing new. **3D TV** peaked at 23% of U.S. TV sales dollars in 2012, then faded so fast that the big makers dropped it by 2017. If an SI washer does exactly what the AI washer did, the new name won't stick.

### The word comes with politics

There's no avoiding the elephant in the room. SI's sudden visibility comes from **Executive Order 14434** (September 29, 2026), which tells federal agencies to say "Super Intelligence" instead of "Artificial Intelligence." It binds the government, not companies.

Industry is split. According to Newsweek, Nvidia's Jensen Huang, Elon Musk, Salesforce's Marc Benioff, and Microsoft's Satya Nadella have adopted the language. Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, and Amazon's AWS still say "AI." For a brand that sells to every kind of household, a word that's become partisan is a reason to wait, not rush.

---

## So When Does SI Show Up?

When there's a new generation of products to put it on. My guess at how it plays out:

1. **It launches as the upgrade.** The next big product cycle introduces SI on something that does a new job. AI suggests. SI **finishes the task**: an assistant that books, buys, and follows up on its own.
2. **It becomes the standard.** A cycle or two later, every model says SI.
3. **"AI" becomes history.** It starts to sound like last generation, the way "HD Ready" does on a TV today.

The first place to look is CES 2027 in January.

---

## What I'm Taking Away

An order can change what the government calls the technology overnight. Companies can't change what's on the box that fast. They'll switch when they have a new product that earns the new name, and then "AI" will quietly become history.

So, a question for you: **what would a product need to do before you'd believe the "SI" on the box?**
