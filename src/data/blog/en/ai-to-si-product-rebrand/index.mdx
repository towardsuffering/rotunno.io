Look closely at the cover. Those are LG washers and dryers, and the badge above them says **"Deep Learning AI DD."** That's artificial intelligence, certified, on a laundry machine.

Ten years ago, "AI" was a word for research labs and science fiction. Now it's on the washer. That's not just a technology story. It's a **product life cycle** story, and the word itself is the product.

So here's my question. A new term is circling: **Super Intelligence, or SI.** Washington has started using it, and so have I (my own title on this site reads *SI Orchestration*). When does SI make it onto the washer? And what has to happen first?

---

## Every Label Has a Life Cycle

Business school teaches four stages for a product: **introduction, growth, maturity, and decline.** Sales start slowly, take off, flatten out, then fade.

Marketing labels follow the same curve, just faster:

- **"Smart"** started as a premium word on phones. Then came smart TVs, smart watches, smart plugs, and smart toothbrushes. Once everything was smart, the word stopped telling you anything.
- **"HD"** was a selling point until every screen had it. The industry didn't fight the curve. It started a new one, called **4K**.
- **"Color TV"** is the best example. Nobody advertises a color TV anymore. When a feature is everywhere, the label quietly disappears, and that's the decline stage.

Labels can also be pushed too far. In December 2017, Long Island Iced Tea Corp. renamed itself **Long Blockchain Corp.** The stock jumped, Nasdaq delisted it about two months later, and the SEC later brought insider trading charges tied to the announcement. A hot label with nothing new behind it doesn't extend a life cycle. It ends one.

---

## Where the AI Label Sits Right Now

Lay "AI" on the curve and it's easy to place.

**Introduction (around 2017 to 2019).** AI showed up as a show-floor novelty. At CES 2018, LG rolled out its **ThinQ AI** brand, and Samsung previewed an 8K TV with "8K AI Technology" that upscaled the picture. It was impressive, a little vague, and mostly premium.

**Growth (2023 to 2025).** Generative AI went mainstream, and the label went into product names. Samsung launched **Galaxy AI** with the Galaxy S24 in January 2024. Microsoft created a PC category, **Copilot+ PC**, that May. Apple answered with **Apple Intelligence**, which conveniently shortens to... AI.

**Maturity (now).** AI is on washers, refrigerators, and air purifiers. Samsung sells **Bespoke AI** appliances, and its CES 2026 theme was "Your Companion to AI Living." When every product in the aisle says AI, the label stops setting anything apart.

Mature brands do the textbook things: tweak the product, find new markets, or **reposition**. LG gave a perfect example of repositioning at CES 2024. Its CEO, William Cho, said LG's AI stands for **"Affectionate Intelligence."** LG kept the two letters everyone recognizes and changed what they mean.

## SI Needs Its Own Curve, Not a New Sticker

Here's where the business side gets interesting. If Samsung swapped "AI" for "SI" on the same fridge tomorrow, what would a shopper see? The same fridge, with a new sticker. SI would skip the introduction stage and land straight in the skepticism, and nobody wins that way.

Companies also have good reasons to keep milking the AI label. Galaxy AI, Copilot+, and Bespoke AI represent years of advertising. In portfolio terms, a mature label is a **cash cow**: you don't throw it away, you keep collecting on it while you fund what's next.

So my educated guess is that SI arrives the way 4K did: as a **new curve on top of the old one**, attached to a capability AI couldn't honestly claim.

- **A new tier first.** Think "Pro" or "Ultra." *Galaxy SI* above *Galaxy AI*, sold as the premium step up, while AI becomes the standard model.
- **A new job, not a better answer.** AI suggests. The first real SI products will probably **finish the task**: an assistant that books, buys, and follows up on its own, or a home that runs itself instead of answering questions about itself.
- **Seeds already planted.** Meta named its lab **Meta Superintelligence Labs** in 2025 and published a vision for "personal superintelligence." When the product is ready, the name is already waiting.

| Stage | What the label tells a buyer | The AI version | What SI will need |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | "This is new and premium" | ThinQ AI, 8K AI TV (2018) | A capability AI can't claim |
| **Growth** | "This is the one to buy" | Galaxy AI, Copilot+ PC (2024) | A tier above AI, not a replacement |
| **Maturity** | "This is standard" | AI washers and fridges (now) | Time, and proof it works |
| **Decline** | Nothing; it's assumed | "Color TV" | Hopefully a long way off |

## What I'll Be Watching

The government's switch (Executive Order 14434, signed September 29, 2026) can change what agencies call the technology. It can't move a product along its life cycle. Only customers can do that. So instead of watching Washington, I'll be watching the next round of launches, starting with CES 2027 in January:

- **Who goes first?** A challenger with nothing to lose, or a giant that wants to own the word?
- **Is SI a name or a tier?** A new product line, or a premium level of an existing one?
- **Does the product earn it?** If the first SI device just does what last year's AI device did, the curve will flatten before it starts.

---

## What I'm Taking Away

As an analyst, I spend a lot of time looking at curves: adoption, revenue, churn. The AI label has the classic shape. It went from novelty to the name on the box to the badge on your washer in under a decade. Labels that reach maturity don't get renamed. They get replaced by something new.

I use SI in my own title because it describes how I work today: I design the system, SI builds it, and I make sure it works. Brands will make the switch when their products can honestly say the same.

So, a question for you: **what would a product need to do before you'd believe the "SI" on the box?**
